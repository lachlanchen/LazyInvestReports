Avoid weak balance sheets, negative gross margin stories, pre-commercial technology without financing visibility, and "AI-adjacent" labels that do not show up in revenue, backlog, free cash flow, or customer evidence.

The maintained single best huge-growth choice remains `TARS` (Tarsus Pharmaceuticals). ## Macro, Rates, and Liquidity

The Fed is on hold, but the hold is not dovish. The June 17 FOMC statement kept the federal funds target range at 3.50%-3.75% and stated that economic activity was expanding at a solid pace while inflation remained elevated relative to the 2% goal ([Federal Reserve FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260617a.htm)). This matters for stock selection because the market can continue to pay for visible growth, but the discount rate remains high enough to punish long-duration cash flows when the evidence weakens.

Rates are moderately constructive for risk assets but not easy. FRED showed the 10-year Treasury constant maturity yield at 4.40% and the 2-year at 4.09% on 2026-06-25, updated on 2026-06-26, leaving a positive but not deeply steep curve ([FRED DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), [FRED DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2)). The front-end funding complex is stable: effective fed funds was 3.63% and SOFR was 3.64% on 2026-06-25 ([FRED DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF), [FRED SOFR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SOFR)). That argues against a liquidity panic, but it does not give weak companies a free refinancing pass.

The inflation data are the biggest reason not to overextend risk. May CPI rose 0.5% month over month and 4.2% year over year, while energy rose 3.9% month over month and 23.5% year over year; core CPI was less alarming at 0.2% month over month and 2.9% year over year ([BLS May CPI](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)). The May PCE release was also hot enough to matter: PCE prices rose 0.4% month over month and 4.1% year over year; core PCE rose 0.3% month over month and 3.4% year over year ([BEA May personal income and outlays](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-may-2026)). The best equity setup is therefore not "buy everything because cuts are coming"; it is "own companies that can compound through a high nominal-rate environment."

The real economy is still functioning. May nonfarm payroll employment increased by 172,000 and the unemployment rate stayed at 4.3%; BLS also noted the unemployment rate had stayed in a narrow 4.3%-4.5% range since July 2025 ([BLS jobs](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)). BEA's Q1 GDP third estimate showed 2.1% annualized real GDP growth, but real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose only 1.7%, which is why I do not want to treat the headline as a green light for economically sensitive leverage ([BEA GDP](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-third-estimate-industries-corporate-profits-state-gdp-and-state-personal-income-1st)).

Consumer sentiment improved but remains low. The University of Michigan final June reading rose to 49.5 from 44.8 in May, while current conditions were 47.7 and expectations were 50.7; the headline index remained below the 60.7 reading from June 2025 ([University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers](https://www.sca.isr.umich.edu/)). That mix supports selective consumer exposure only when pricing power or membership economics are visible.

Manufacturing is one of the better macro supports for industrial and power-infrastructure names. ISM reported May manufacturing PMI at 54.0, the highest reading in the 12-month table shown in the release, with new orders at 56.8 and production at 54.3. The warning is that the Prices Index stayed very high at 82.1, driven by steel, aluminum, tariffs, and petroleum-based products ([ISM May PMI](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-54-may-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302786165.html)). This is good for companies with pricing power and backlog quality, but dangerous for fixed-price contractors and thin-margin manufacturers.

### Why Not the Other Watchlist Names

`YOU` is higher quality on current free cash flow, but its growth/asymmetry is lower. `VCEL` is also high quality, but it is a steadier medtech compounding story rather than a potential category-creation breakout. `GENI` has strong revenue growth and raised guidance, but the Legend acquisition adds integration, leverage, and non-GAAP reliance. `CECO` and `WLDN` are attractive infrastructure beneficiaries, but they are integration and project-execution stories. `ALKT` is good vertical SaaS but still GAAP unprofitable and exposed to bank software budgets. `BELFB` is a useful AI-infrastructure-adjacent component idea, but its revenue growth is more cyclical and the May offering adds share-count and use-of-proceeds monitoring.

### Bear Case

The bear case is straightforward: XDEMVY adoption slows, payer friction rises, gross-to-net gets worse, SG&A remains too high, or the market decides the peak-sales bridge is less credible than management believes. Tarsus also has one-product concentration risk. Pipeline assets can help the story, but they should not be used to excuse a miss in the core XDEMVY commercial ramp.

### Falsification Checklist

`TARS` loses the single best huge-growth slot if any of the following happen:

- Full-year XDEMVY guidance is reduced or management shifts from confidence to defensive language.
- Quarterly XDEMVY sales growth materially decelerates without a clear temporary explanation.
- Gross-to-net discounts worsen enough to challenge the category economics.
- SG&A growth consumes the product gross margin bridge and delays a credible profitability path.
- New safety, reimbursement, regulatory, or competitive evidence changes the expected peak-sales curve.
- Insider-sale, share issuance, or debt activity becomes large enough to change the risk/reward rather than merely the optics.
- Another maintained watchlist name delivers cleaner growth with better free cash flow and lower concentration risk.
